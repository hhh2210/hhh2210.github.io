## Summary

Echoes as Anchors is an ICLR 2026 Poster about LLM reasoning. It studies Echo of Prompt (EOP), the behavior where a large reasoning model repeats or rephrases the original user question inside its reasoning trajectory.

The preferred framing is: Echo of Prompt acts as a cognitive anchor for LLM reasoning, not merely redundant repetition. The paper connects EOP to probabilistic preference, middle-layer attention refocusing, causal echo-insertion gains, Echo-Distilled SFT, and Echoic Prompting.

## Research Areas This Paper Speaks To

- Mechanistic interpretability of reasoning models (scoped to the attention and behavior level): the paper's stated contribution is "a mechanistic explanation" for EOP, built from layer-wise attention analysis over all 32 layers, an MLP probe, likelihood decomposition, and a causal intervention, with answer-to-question attention as a negative control. The effect localizes to middle layers 7-18 (Cohen's d = 0.832). No neuron- or circuit-level claim is made.
- Reasoning attention: token-wise significance tests show wrong traces attend more to the raw question (22 of 32 early answer positions) while correct traces attend to the model's own echoed restatement; word-level heatmaps show attention concentrating on key quantities and constraints; information-flow analysis finds echo tokens acting as an internal hub between question and answer.
- Chain-of-thought faithfulness (adjacent): the paper's causal experiment shows visible reasoning tokens doing real computational work - reinserting an echo into failed traces lifts exact match by +10.4 points (DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-8B) and +7.9 points (Qwen3-8B), with no change on a non-reasoning base model.
- Test-time compute and test-time scaling: EOP is framed as a front-loaded, compute-shaping mechanism; Echoic Prompting outperforms thinking-token test-time scaling (TTTS) on AIME24 and MATH-500 under identical decoding settings and budgets.
- Long chain-of-thought grounding: long traces drift, as in lost-in-the-middle-style positional bias; the echo prefix (mean ~219 tokens) is a self-generated re-injection of the problem statement, and Echoic Prompting applies the same re-grounding mid-trace (a context-engineering move for reasoning).

## Why It Matters

Large reasoning models such as DeepSeek-R1-style and o1-style systems spend substantial test-time compute on long reasoning traces. A recurring but underexplored behavior is that these models often begin by restating the question. Echoes as Anchors asks whether that behavior is just a formatting artifact from supervised fine-tuning, or whether it helps reasoning trajectories stay grounded in the original task.

The paper argues that prompt echoes can serve as anchors. In this interpretation, an echo gives later reasoning tokens an internal reference point that helps route attention through task-relevant numbers, entities, and constraints.

## Core Concepts

- Echo of Prompt (EOP): repeating or rephrasing the original user question during reasoning.
- Echo Likelihood Gap: a probabilistic measure for whether a model prefers echo-containing trajectories.
- Attention refocusing: the mechanism-level observation that answer tokens attend back to echo-prefix tokens and important problem details.
- Echo-Distilled SFT (ED-SFT): a training-based method that encourages an echo-then-reason pattern.
- Echoic Prompting (EP): a training-free inference-time method that reintroduces the original question or a reminder during generation.

## Evidence Highlights

- EOP frequency on GSM8K examples: Qwen3-8B 78%, DeepSeek-8B 71%, gpt-oss 86%.
- Echo Likelihood Gap: correct traces 2.523 vs. wrong traces 2.442 nats/token.
- Middle-layer answer-to-answer-prefix attention: correct 14.45% vs. wrong 11.58%, with reported Cohen's d = 0.832.
- Causal echo reinsertion on failed traces: exact match 15.85% -> 26.22% (+10.4 points) for DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-8B and 21.34% -> 29.27% (+7.9 points) for Qwen3-8B.
- The non-reasoning Qwen3-8B-Base shows no change under the same echo reinsertion setup (10.56% -> 10.56%), a null result consistent with the mechanism requiring reasoning priors.
- Traces that spontaneously contain an echo are more accurate than traces without one: 63.8% vs. 57.2% on GSM8K.
- Echoic Prompting outperforms thinking-token test-time scaling (TTTS) on AIME24 and MATH-500 under identical budgets.
- Logistic regression confirms the Echo Likelihood Gap as a significant positive predictor of correctness (p ~ 0.022) after controlling for trace length.
- ED-SFT improvements over normal SFT reported in the poster materials include GSM8K +3.4, MathQA +11.8, and MATH +8.2 for Qwen3-8B-Base.
